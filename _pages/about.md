Welcome to the Crustal Deformation and InSAR Geodesy lab at Universidad de Chile!

I am an InSAR geodesist from Chile and assistant professor at the Department of Geology of Universidad de Chile. 

My research focuses on the use of space geodetic data to understand a variety of crustal deformation processes that occur in volcanoes, faults, plate boundaries, glaciers, and salars. Specifically I use InSAR (Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar), GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System), and DEM (Digital Elevation Models) that I complement with field observations, seismological, geochemical, and mineralogical data. Most of my research have focused to understand magma storage, transport, and eruption dynamics in volcanoes in subdution zones (Southern Andes, Aleutians, Vanuatu) and hot spots (Yellowstone, Galapagos, Hawai'i). In the past years I have worked mostly to understand the strain accumulation, release and the segmentation of active faults in the Andean fold and thrust belt and the Magallanes Fagnano fault zone, the plate boundary between the Sotuh America and Scotia plates. You can check out a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hgulqg3zjC4) of the research of carried out by my lab.
